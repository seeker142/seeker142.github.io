<h2>Chapter 2: Things She Likes</h2>

The commercial district of Sazandol was divided into eastern and western sections by the main thoroughfare. The western side, closer to the harbor, was crowded with a jumble of food stalls and street vendors, while the eastern side had a more subdued atmosphere, with many high-end shops.

Monica had been living in Sazandol for over half a year, but she had rarely visited the street lined with luxury shops. Somehow, she always felt out of place there.

On that very street, Monica stepped out of the carriage alongside Lana.

"All right, Rick. Come pick us up when it's time."

"Yes, Miss! Take your time!"

"Thank you. I'll buy you lots of souvenirs."

The young coachman, Rick, blushed and nodded vigorously.

The street was filled with elegantly dressed people strolling along and chatting gracefully. It was bustling, but not crowded. It was a refined kind of bustle, befitting a street lined with high-end shops.

Before coming here, Monica had changed into a neat outfit. Lana had also fixed her previously disheveled hair.

When Lana had asked her what kind of hairstyle she wanted, Monica had fidgeted shyly before answering:

"Something like yours, Lana... from when we were students..."

Lana had smiled brightly, then neatly arranged Monica's hair in a cute style and done her own hair to match.

Seeing Lana with that nostalgic hairstyle made Monica's heart flutter as she remembered their school days. And having a matching hairstyle with Lana made her heart flutter, too. It was amazing how excited she could get over something as simple as a hairstyle. Lana really was incredible.

[image]

"First, let's go look for a new cup. There's a shop I always go to."

The place Lana led her to was an upscale tableware shop.

When the two girls, sporting matching hairstyles, stepped inside, an elderly shopkeeper with a refined air greeted them with a gentle smile.

"Welcome. Well, well, if it isn't Colette-sama. Have you brought a friend with you today?"

"Yes. Could you show us your teacups?"

"Certainly. This way, please."

Lana followed the shopkeeper with a confident air. Monica, feeling fidgety in the unfamiliar shop, followed along behind her.

Shelves for displaying the merchandise lined the back of the store. They made lavish use of highly transparent plate glass, and their edges were decorated with ornate carvings. Even the shelves themselves gave off an air of luxury. Arranged in neat rows upon them was a dazzling assortment of beautiful teacups.

"...They're pretty."

When Monica murmured those words, the shopkeeper smiled happily, the wrinkles at the corners of his eyes deepening.

"Indeed. Aren't they all beautiful?"

Some were decorated with wildflowers painted in pale colors, others featured incredibly intricate designs despite using only a single color, and still others depicted exotic birds and plants. Every single one looked like a work of art.

Lana began enthusiastically explaining the cups.

"That one is a new piece from the Lemaire Collection. The Lemaire Collection is known for its raised gold paint. See? It's slightly embossed. The vivid colors and gold paint give it such a luxurious, lovely look."

"Mm-hmm. It's really quite gorgeous."

"Right, right? And these cups with their incredibly intricate designs are made by transferring copperplate engravings! The technology has improved recently, so even the finest lines can now be reproduced beautifully..."

As she talked about teacups, Lana seemed almost unusually excited.

Maybe it was just Monica's imagination, but Lana was probably going to such lengths to cheer her up.

(What should I do? I'm so happy.)

Monica wasn't as interested in fashion or shopping as Lana was.

Even so, she was enjoying dressing up and shopping like this because she was happy about Lana's kindness and her efforts to cheer Monica up.

(...Maybe I should buy a teacup.)

The tea set Lana had given her consisted of six cups, and Monica had broken one of them.

She still had five, so there was no urgent need to replace the broken one. Nevertheless, for the first time in a while, Monica felt like buying something for herself.

(These cute little flowers... And this blue one is pretty, too...)

After carefully examining the cups one by one, Monica stopped in front of a section displaying mugs for everyday use.

The white porcelain mugs were decorated with black cats sleeping among wildflowers. Perhaps they were part of a series, because there were several different designs: black cats playing with butterflies, leaving paw prints as they walked, and so on.

"...It looks like Nero."

The words slipped out before she could stop them, and Lana reacted.

"Nero? That black cat I sometimes see at your house?"

"Eh? Ah, um... yes. He's my... well, my familiar..."

Monica stammered, her gaze wandering.

At present, only Monica and Isaac knew the true identity of the black cat who was hibernating.

Naturally, ordinary cats didn't hibernate. So Monica kept the basket Nero was sleeping in inside her bedroom, where visitors wouldn't see it.

She was afraid that if anyone pressed her about Nero, she'd end up letting something slip.

Fortunately, Lana didn't pursue the subject any further. She simply looked at the mug Monica had been examining and murmured, "It really does look just like him."

Monica took another good look at the mugs.

A cat sleeping among wildflowers, a cat playing with butterflies, a cat taking a walk and leaving paw prints, a cat batting at a floating leaf on the surface of the water...

(I want to show these mugs to Nero and Ike, too.)

They weren't exactly the sort of design she'd use when entertaining guests, but they might be nice for everyday use.

All right! Mustering her resolve, Monica called out to the shopkeeper.

"I'll take this mug, please!"

After arranging to have her purchase delivered to her home, they browsed some accessory shops and the like. Before long, they began to feel a little hungry, so the two headed toward the fried-pie shop that had been their original destination.

Apparently, Lana's favorite shop was a food stall in the western district.

"If you're going to that food stall, it's faster to take this road. But absolutely don't come this way after dark. It's not a particularly safe area, and if you go one street farther in, you'll be in the back alleys."

For a wealthy young lady to go out alone without an attendant in the first place was enough to make people raise their eyebrows. And Sazandol was hardly a safe town. A bustling, lively port town saw all sorts of people coming and going, and among them were criminals.

Lana seemed to know even better than Monica which roads were safe to walk alone and at what times. After glancing around, Lana stopped and lowered her voice to whisper to Monica.

"Above all, don't go anywhere near the back of the western district. I've heard rumors that merchants dealing in some nasty drugs have been coming and going there lately... If any foreigners approach you around that area, run away immediately."

"Y-yes."

Monica obediently nodded at Lana's warning.

Technically, Monica was the foremost sorceress in the kingdom and a practitioner of chantless magic. She had even shot down an entire flock of wyverns in an instant.

Even so, she was happy that Lana cared enough to worry about her so naturally.

Monica was just about to say, "Thank you for worrying about me," when a loud voice rang out behind them.

"Thief!!"

At the booming voice, Monica and Lana instinctively turned around.

A middle-aged man was running toward them from the direction of the shout. He held a knife in his right hand and a large travel bag in his left. This man was presumably the thief.

Chasing after him was a large, black-haired man who looked like a traveler. However, there was quite a distance between them, so if the thief managed to escape down a side street, it would be difficult to catch him.

"Out of the way! Out of the wayyy!"

The thief approached them, wildly swinging the knife in his right hand.

Lana turned pale and grabbed Monica's arm.

"Monica, look out!"

Lana tried to pull Monica toward the side of the road. But Monica didn't move, keeping her eyes fixed straight ahead on the thief.

The expression vanished from her still-youthful face.

"...It's all right."

Without chanting, Monica deployed a defensive barrier directly in front of the thief.

The thief failed to notice the transparent wall that had suddenly appeared and crashed straight into it. "Bwagh!" he screamed, falling flat on his backside.

"What the hell is this...? I just hit something..."

As the thief groaned and clutched his injured nose, a thick arm suddenly shot out from behind him. It belonged to the large man who had been chasing him.

"—Hmph!"

The man's thick arm clamped around the thief's neck. His muscular biceps bulged so prominently that they were visible even through his clothes, restraining the thief like iron shackles.

The thief flailed his arms and legs for a while, but before long, his eyes rolled back and his body convulsed. Then he went limp.

The black-haired giant tossed the thief onto the ground, let out a breath, and wiped the sweat from his forehead.

Up close, he was an extremely large man with a rugged face. His black hair was short, and he had a beard on his chin. He looked to be in his late twenties.

The man looked back and forth between Monica and Lana before speaking.

"Ladies! Are you injured?!"

His voice was so ridiculously loud that one might have wanted to tell him there was no need to shout quite so loudly at such close range.

Monica, startled by the booming voice, trembled with tears in her eyes. Lana stepped forward with an awkward smile.

"Indeed, we're unharmed, Mister. Are those shoes yours?"

"No, to be precise, they're not mine, but my younger brother's..."

The man had gotten that far when he turned around and shouted angrily behind him.

"Teodor! Why are you dawdling along so slowly?!"

In the direction of his gaze, a young man was strolling leisurely toward them.

He was a slender young man with soft-looking, fluffy black hair. His eyes drooped slightly, giving him a somewhat sleepy look, and perhaps because of that, he seemed to be smiling faintly. He looked to be around twenty years old.

The sleepy-looking young man stopped in front of Monica and the others, looked up at the large man, and spoke in a gentle, unhurried voice.
