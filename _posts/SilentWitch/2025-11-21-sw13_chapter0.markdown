<h2>Prologue: Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans</h2>

*Machine translated from the first 60 pages of the Bookwalker preview.*

---

Karina was a girl living in the Empire of Schvargart who loved making things.

Her greatest talent was metal engraving, but she also enjoyed woodworking and glasswork. Even looking at blueprints for buildings and ships made her heart race. In short, she simply loved creating things.

Being able to make things was happiness itself. There was nothing in the world quite so wonderful.

However, making something good required equipment and materials. What was more, in the region where the girl lived, women were not allowed to become craftsmen. The best Karina could manage was making things with her craftsman brother's tools and leftover materials.

Then, one day, the head of the workshop where her brother worked agreed to hire Karina on the condition that she dress as a man. On top of that, the workshop head even prepared a workspace exclusively for her. Karina was so overjoyed she could have leaped into the air, and she threw herself into making the commissioned items.

For example, a brooch modeled after a bird's wing. She paid particular attention to the smooth, delicate lines of the intricate goldwork.

For example, a necklace for a noblewoman. She struggled to recreate the old-fashioned style, from its decorations down to its clasp.

For example, a jewel box with mirrors lining its interior. She carefully adjusted the angles of the mirrors so that it wouldn't feel cramped despite its limited capacity.

Making every one of them was fun. The more intricate the craftsmanship, the more her heart burned with excitement. So engrossed was she in the joy of creating that Karina failed to notice.

The envy directed at her, and the greed of those who sought to exploit her.

If Karina had desired fame, or if she had cared about how people judged the things she made, perhaps the situation would have turned out differently.

But Karina was satisfied as long as she could make things that met her own standards. She had no interest in how her works were evaluated.

That was why she never realized what she was being made to create.

Several years after she began working at the workshop, the emperor was succeeded by a new ruler. The new emperor was known as the Black Lion Emperor, so Karina thought that commissions for ornaments featuring lions would probably increase.

Then, one day, Karina was arrested. Her charge was fraud: producing counterfeit goods and selling them as genuine articles. Karina had simply made the things the workshop head asked her to make, exactly as instructed. It was the workshop head who had sold them. But all the crimes were pinned on Karina alone.

(Ohhh, so I'd been tricked.)

She felt no anger toward the workshop head. Karina had been able to make whatever she wanted until she was satisfied. That alone meant she had been incredibly fortunate.

There was just one thing she felt sorry about: causing trouble for her family.

Her mother and brother must surely be saddened. It was her brother who had taught her metal engraving. Perhaps he might even blame himself. That was the only thing weighing on her heart.

The fraud case involving the counterfeits Karina had made had caused enormous financial damages, and her execution by hanging was all but certain.

All Karina could do now was wait for death in her prison cell.

Karina's neighboring cell already had an occupant. He was a thin man with long, steel-colored hair, and his name was Helmfried Kollwitz. He was a famous lace-maker in the Empire, but he was quite the eccentric.

He treated his creations like his own daughters, and anyone who dirtied or insulted them was subjected to an absolutely vicious verbal assault. The reason he had been imprisoned was that he had dragged down the former emperor's mistress and verbally abused her in the foulest terms after she spilled wine on his lace.

He loved his creations far too much. As a craftsman, he was the exact opposite of Karina, who had no interest in what happened to her works after she finished them.

"Kollwitz-san, Kollwitz-san, what are you doing right now?"

"I'm making lace out of my hair."

Kollwitz's hair was long, straight, and steel-colored. The lace he was making with it was the sort of thing that seemed as though it might be steeped in resentment.

Suddenly, Karina had a question.

"Are you allowed to bring knitting needles in here?"

"No. I'm making it with my fingers alone. If you bite your fingernails with your teeth to make them jagged, they're easier to catch the thread on."

It was probably a technique that the vast majority of people would go their entire lives without ever needing.

On the other side of the single wall separating their cells, a thin man was plucking out his own hair strand by strand, then using his fingers and nails to weave lace. His dedication was incredible.

"What kind of piece are you making right now?"

"This is not a finished piece. I'm merely working out the concept for my next creation."

"Kollwitz-san, do you think you'll be able to get out?"

"I don't know. But if I do get out, I'll make lace. This time, I'll dedicate the finest lace to someone truly worthy of my work."

Karina wondered what she would do if she were ever allowed to leave.

In her imagination, she was still making one thing or another, without choosing her customers.

"Kollwitz-san, you choose your customers, don't you?"

"Of course. I'm marrying off my precious daughter, after all. Naturally, I must choose carefully."

He called his lace his beloved daughter and even described entrusting it to someone as marrying her off. His devotion was on an entirely different level.

Karina was genuinely impressed by Kollwitz's dedication.

Karina would be happy if someone treasured and made good use of the things she created, but that was all. She had no attachment or obsession toward her own works. Simply making them was enough to satisfy her.

(...So that's why I got caught.)

As she hugged her knees, having come to terms with it, she heard footsteps. The familiar footsteps of the guards she heard every day, and another set. Heavy, deep, unhurried footsteps.

Karina raised her head almost at the same moment the footsteps stopped.

"Karina Barr, the counterfeiter."

The one who called Karina's name was a black-haired man standing on the other side of the iron bars. He had deep-set features and a ruggedly masculine face. He was well-built, too. He looked like someone who would make an impressive sculpture.

The man casually draped a coat adorned with luxurious fur and beautiful tassels over his shoulders, looking down at Karina. A small jewel box rested in his hand.

"I hear you're the one who made this. Is that true?"

"Mm-hmm, that's right. I made that one."

Karina took one look at the jewel box and nodded.

It was a jewel box with mirrors lining the interior and a double bottom—in other words, it had a hidden compartment.

"It's exceptionally well made. Above all, the ingenuity behind making it appear to have a larger capacity is remarkable."

"Wah-ha! You noticed that? That's right! Jewel boxes with double bottoms have less storage space because of the hidden compartment, right? So I came up with a way to make it look like it has more room than it actually does, without anyone noticing the hidden compartment~!"

As Karina launched into a lengthy explanation, the guard stepped forward with a grim expression. He was probably trying to silence her, but the black-haired man stopped him with a wave of one hand.

"Leave her be."

"But..."

"Enough. I said leave her be."

After silencing the guard, the man fixed his gaze on Karina.

His eyes were so powerful that, for some reason, she couldn't look away. I wonder what it would be like to make a statue of him, Karina thought in the back of her mind.

"According to the investigation, you were able to accurately reproduce almost every item after seeing it just once. Is that true?"

"Yep! Once I've seen a pattern, color, or shape, I usually never forget it. Wah-ha! Though I tend to forget things people tell me. I can't even remember how many times the workshop head got mad at me for forgetting deadlines..."

The man silently listened to Karina's chatter. If anything, his expression even seemed somewhat amused.

"Do you know what this jewel box is?"

"Nope. I don't know, and I'm not interested."

What interested Karina were colors, shapes, designs, and things of that nature. She had little interest in an object's origins or value.

"I see. This mirror box is even better made than the original."

The man began speaking as though he knew the original personally.

"The mirror box of the master craftsman Obermeyer. It is one of the treasures of Kreving, which was sold off during the previous emperor's reign. When I was a child, I played a prank on it, so the hidden lid on the original no longer opens."

Kreving. Even Karina, who knew very little about the world around her, had heard of it.

More importantly, there was probably only one person in the entire Empire who referred to himself as "Yo."

"I am Leonhart Alois Maximilian Bernd Kreving."

The man glanced toward the neighboring cell. Knowing Kollwitz, he was probably completely absorbed in making lace—or rather, weaving his own hair—and hadn't paid the slightest attention to their exchange.

"Karina Barr, the counterfeit artisan. Helmfried Kollwitz, the lace artisan. I hereby appoint you both as members of the Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans, magic technology advisors serving directly under the Emperor."

The man smiled, showing his white teeth. His smile was both cheerful and ferocious, like that of a large predatory beast.

"Put your skills to use in the name of the Black Lion."

On a clear winter day, Karina was making her way toward Helmfried Kollwitz's workshop. She wore simple work clothes with an inexpensive coat thrown over them. Karina was nineteen years old that year, but she was petite and wore no makeup, so she looked somewhat younger than her actual age.

Along the way, a heap of snow tumbled down from a tree and landed on her black hair, which was tied up in a bun. Karina brushed it away with her fingertips, then spent a moment admiring the snow clinging to her sleeve. She was wondering what sort of design would be fun to carve if she used the shape of a snowflake as its motif.

It might be fun to engrave it incredibly, incredibly small on a silver plate, so tiny that one would have to look closely to see it.

As she was thinking about that, more snow fell.

Once Karina became absorbed in her thoughts, she wouldn't even close her eyes when snow fell on her. She simply stared intently at the snowflakes clinging to her bangs... though, eventually, the cold became too much.

"Ah-choo! ...Brrr, it's freezing..."

Karina sniffled, carefully brushed the snow off her hair and clothes, and entered Kollwitz's workshop.

"Kollwitz-san, gooood afternoon!"

When she opened the door to the workroom, she found the workshop's owner dancing with a completely expressionless face.

He alternately thrust his clenched fists toward the sky while stomping his feet. Karina wasn't sure whether those movements could really be called dancing, but that's what they looked like to her. And in fact, Kollwitz was expressing his joy with his entire body.

Kollwitz was a tall, slender man who was forty years old that year. His steel-colored hair reached down to his back and was loosely tied together, and he wore round glasses over his rather nervous-looking face.

He was dressed in work clothes with narrow sleeves, and bits of thread were stuck all over them. He must have been working right up until that very moment.

Spread out on the worktable in front of Kollwitz was a beautiful lace stole. It was pure white overall, like snow, but silver thread had been used in several places to depict magical formulas. They blended naturally into the lace pattern, creating perfect harmony.

"Wah-ha!! Don't tell me you've finished a new lace creation?! Let me see, let me see, let me seeee!"

As Karina innocently approached the worktable, Kollwitz, who had been performing his joyful dance, suddenly widened his eyes and shouted furiously.

"Don't touch my 'Dietlinde' with those filthy hands!"

Karina flinched like a cat being scolded and raised both hands into the air.

"I'm not touching it~. Just looking, just looking. Kollwitz-san, did you give another one of your creations a name?"

"What's wrong with giving a name to the daughter I brought into this world with every ounce of my heart and soul?"

Kollwitz was a genius craftsman said to have no equal in the Empire when it came to lace and embroidery, but he had a habit of cherishing his creations as though they were his own daughters.

He had taken that habit too far and ended up imprisoned for it. Though, when it came to criminal records, Karina wasn't exactly in a position to criticize him.

"Is this little 'Dietlinde' going to be presented to His Majesty?"

"Yes. Apparently, it's going to be a stole for Cecilia-sama."

Kollwitz gazed at the lace called 'Dietlinde' spread across his worktable and let out an enraptured sigh.

"There is no woman in this country who suits lace as well as Cecilia-sama. This delicate and charming 'Dietlinde' is perfectly suited to a princess with such a pure heart. I'm sure 'Dietlinde' will be happy to become Cecilia-sama's bride, too."

Princess Cecilia was the younger sister of the current emperor, Leonhart. Kollwitz was a huge fan of Princess Cecilia. Apparently, whenever he looked at her, ideas for new lace creations came pouring into his mind.

More than anything, Princess Cecilia treated Kollwitz Lace with the utmost care. That must have made him especially happy.

"You're right, it looks like it'd suit Cecilia-sama perfectly~. Eeehee! It's so cuuute!"

"The magical formulas woven into it are also impeccable. I commend you."

The remark came from directly behind them. It sounded almost like someone talking to themselves, but the voice carried clearly.

Karina turned around like a startled cat to find the black-haired man standing behind her. He had his arms crossed and was grinning.

The man had no attendants with him, but he couldn't conceal the overwhelming aura of authority radiating from his entire body.

It was the Black Lion Emperor, Leonhart.

The moment she recognized him, Karina quickly exchanged a glance with Kollwitz. Now was the time to unveil the special routine they had been diligently practicing.

After confirming Kollwitz's slight nod, Karina swiftly stepped in front of him.

She spread her feet a little wider than shoulder-width apart, thrust her right hand toward the ceiling, and shouted.

"Raise the crimson furnace to the heavens, artisans who engrave the pinnacle of craftsmanship!"

Standing behind Karina, Kollwitz placed his right hand against his glasses, stretched his left arm straight out to the side, and raised his voice.

"Four crimson stars illuminating the future of the Empire!"

Karina brought the hand she had raised toward the heavens down to her eye, keeping her fingers spread so that she could peer through the gaps.

At the same moment, Kollwitz crossed both arms in front of his body.

"Barr of the Divine Eye!"

"Kollwitz of the Fairy's Fingers!"

"And together, we are the Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans!"

The two of them announced their title in unison for the grand finale.

One of Kollwitz's apprentices, who had happened to come by to collect the garbage, quietly walked past the doorway with the expression of someone who had just witnessed something they were never supposed to see.

The Black Lion Emperor nodded with a deeply satisfied expression.

"Indeed. A powerful and splendid introduction. Your movements are sharp, too. However, that pose seems to assume all four members are present, does it not? With only two of you, the balance is somewhat off."

At the Black Lion Emperor's observation, Kollwitz bowed his head with complete seriousness.

"My apologies, Your Majesty. We shall consider a performance better suited to our numbers."

"Hey, hey, Kollwitz-san. What if, at that part, instead of raising my hand upward, I stretch it out to the right? Wouldn't that look nice and symmetrical?"

"No. The difference in our arm lengths is too great, so that would throw off the balance. It wouldn't be beautiful. Why don't we deliberately make use of our difference in height? I could raise my arm upward while you extend yours downward, creating a tall, vertical silhouette."

"Oh, and another thing! Props! There are still lots of people who wonder what the Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans actually are, right? So, wouldn't it look the part if we held things like tools or spools of thread, to make it obvious that we're a group of artisans?"

"An excellent idea. Let us begin selecting our props immediately."

"I'm going to hold chisels! Wouldn't it look cool if I held a few between my fingers?"

"Just make sure you don't drop them on my feet."

The Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans. It was an extremely imposing name, but in essence, they were magic technology advisors directly under the Emperor.

Apparently, there had been quite an argument between the Black Lion Emperor and his aides over the name, which hardly sounded like something suited to a group of artisans. In the end, however, the matter was settled by the Black Lion Emperor's simple declaration:

"It's fine! It sounds powerful!"

Why the Emperor had decided that artisans needed to sound powerful was something that left everyone scratching their heads. Karina, however, was thoroughly satisfied, declaring, "It's kind of cool!"

Incidentally, the Black Lion Emperor had also ordered them to prepare a dramatic catchphrase and pose for their appearances, saying that they should come up with an appropriate presentation. Karina had approached that task with just as much enthusiasm, once again thinking, "It's kind of cool!"

By the way, besides Karina and Kollwitz, there were two other members of the Imperial Magic Cross Flame Heaven Four Artisans.

Kollwitz was surprisingly enthusiastic about this sort of thing for a middle-aged man, and he gave his all when practicing their dramatic poses. The other two members, however, were rather reluctant when it came to this kind of performance.

"Leaving aside improvements to our presentation for another time... Your Majesty, what brings you here today?"

In response to Kollwitz's question, the Black Lion Emperor nodded and dramatically spread open the coat draped over his shoulders.

"Kollwitz. I hereby command you to create the veil for my younger sister Cecilia's wedding dress."

"What?!"

Karina's upturned eyes widened.

She had never heard anything about Princess Cecilia being engaged. Presumably, the matter was being arranged in secret.

Beside Karina, whose eyes were sparkling, Kollwitz removed his glasses and pressed his fingers against the corners of his eyes.

"To think that I would be entrusted with creating Cecilia-sama's veil... What an extraordinary honor!"

Kollwitz put his glasses back on, placed a hand against his chest, and made his declaration.

"I shall weave the highest-grade defensive barrier into it, so that Cecilia-sama may be protected from every conceivable obstacle. It shall be resistant to both heat and cold, ensuring that the bride remains comfortable whether the wedding ceremony takes place under the blazing summer sun or in the depths of winter. I swear to create a veil that places the bride's happiness and comfort above all else!"

A bridal veil woven with the highest-grade defensive barrier was certainly a rather incomprehensible creation. But craftsmen often had mysterious obsessions that ordinary people simply couldn't understand.

The Black Lion Emperor nodded magnanimously.

"I look forward to it."

Then he turned his gaze toward Karina. His expression seemed to combine ferocity with just a hint of playful mischief.

He was undoubtedly plotting something fun.

"By the way, Karina. You are currently free from work, are you not?"

Just as the Black Lion Emperor had said, Karina had recently finished a major project. At the moment, she was waiting for her next assignment while pursuing her hobby of studying wood carving. Her latest favorite technique was engraving extremely tiny magical formulas along the sides of thin wooden carvings.

Karina instinctively straightened her back a little, and the Black Lion Emperor announced in his resonant voice:

"Karina, I command you in the name of the Black Lion! Use the technical cooperation agreement between the Empire and the Kingdom of Ridill, and proceed to the magical tool workshop in Ambard, the pinnacle of the large-scale tool industry!"
