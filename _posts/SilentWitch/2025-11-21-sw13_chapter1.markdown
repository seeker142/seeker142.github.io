<h2>Chapter 1: The Rich Young Lady Method of Coping When Nothing Goes Right</h2>

One day in midwinter, in the middle of the month, a man came to visit Lana Colette, president of the Flax Trading Company.

The man's name was Clifford. He was the son of one of Lana's father's business associates, and he had visited Lana's home several times. In other words, he was her childhood friend.

He was four years older than Lana. She had heard that he had gone to study economics at a university in the maritime nation of Alpatra, but apparently he had returned to the Kingdom of Ridill just recently.

"Long time no see, Lana."

Clifford spoke in a flat tone, without the slightest hint of nostalgia at their reunion. Lana was quite accustomed to her childhood friend's manner of speaking, so she had no intention of commenting on it.

There were, after all, countless other things she wanted to point out.

First, his unruly black hair, which clearly hadn't seen a comb in days.

Second, his old-fashioned shirt with a pleated collar, which made her want to interrogate him about where he'd bought it.

Third, his unkempt beard, which had clearly grown simply because he'd left it alone, with no regard whatsoever for style.

Fourth, the most atrocious pair of thick-lensed glasses imaginable, which had taken first place in Lana's mental ranking of ugly glasses, knocking Bernie Jones off his throne.

And then...

"Will you marry me."

Fifth, an utterly out-of-the-blue statement.

Suppressing her urge to yell at him right then and there, Lana forced a composed, mature smile onto her face.

"I'm afraid I don't understand what you mean, Mister?"

With that, Lana smiled sweetly, looking utterly unruffled. It was a perfect display of mature decorum.

Clifford let out an ostentatious sigh.

"I chose the most conventional and straightforward way of proposing, and you still don't understand what I mean? You're as stupid as ever, Lana."

"H-huh?"

A blue vein bulged at Lana's temple as she slapped her palm against the table. There wasn't a trace of her earlier composure left.

"Why on earth would I have to marry you, Cliff?! And can't you do something about your hair, clothes, beard, and glasses?!"

Having finally voiced all the complaints she'd been holding back, Lana received a perfectly impassive, matter-of-fact reply from Clifford.

"I'll only answer the question of why we have to get married. 'Because we promised.' Period."

Lana felt an overwhelming urge to tear at her hair.

It was true that, when they were children, Clifford had once told her, "Let's get married when we grow up." That had happened, yes, but it was just a childish statement between two children.

She wanted to yell. She really, really wanted to yell, but she was now the head of a trading company. Losing her temper and taking it out on everyone around her was absolutely out of the question.

Lana desperately calmed herself down, then spoke with a haughty, aloof expression.

"Please go home. I have a regular meeting of the Merchants' Guild to attend."

"That one where Mr. Seymour serves as a board member? Understood. I'll go in your place."

"Why would you be going in my place, Cliff?!"

"Here."

Clifford took a letter from inside his coat and held it out to her.

Lana eyed the letter suspiciously, but when she saw the writing on the envelope, her eyes flew open. The sender was none other than her own father.

*Dear Lana,*

*How are you? I hope you haven't caught a cold or anything. Are you working hard?*

*There are some dangerous areas in Sazandol, so I'm so worried about you that I can't sleep at night.*

*When I discussed this with a friend, imagine my surprise! His son, Clifford-kun, who recently returned from studying abroad, offered to become your secretary.*

*I hear Clifford-kun studied economics at a university in Alpatra. I'm sure he'll be a great help to you. More than anything, I thought having Clifford-kun, who was always so close to you as a child, by your side would give you peace of mind.*

*Keep working hard. I'm rooting for you.*

*With love, to my dear daughter.*

As Lana stood frozen, clutching the letter, Clifford declared in his flat voice:

"And so, starting today, I'll be your secretary. And while we're at it, let's get married."

"Don't propose marriage as an afterthought! Oh, honestly, Father... Why Clifford...?"

Lana slumped back against her chair and gazed up at the ceiling.

There was absolutely no way she intended to marry this oddly dressed childhood friend of hers.

The secretary matter was another story. Lana couldn't simply dismiss her father's kindness.

"...Fine. Since this is Father's recommendation, I'll accept you as my secretary. But I refuse to marry you."

"That's a breach of contract."

"It was a childhood promise... Anyway!"

Lana suppressed her exasperated expression, fixed Clifford with a sharp glare, and pointed a finger straight at him.

"If you're going to be my secretary, the first thing you need to do is something about that unkempt appearance of yours! Comb your hair! Shave your beard! Your glasses... Well, those can wait for now. At the very least, let's do something about your clothes. What is that pleated-collar shirt? Where on earth did you find it...?"

"An antique shop."

Clifford's answer made Lana feel faint, and she staggered.

She'd thought it looked old-fashioned, but she hadn't imagined it was an actual antique!

"Then I'll make this your entrance exam. You must dress in a manner befitting a secretary of the Flax Trading Company. If you don't meet the passing standard, naturally, you won't be hired."

After declaring this firmly, Lana grabbed her shoes and stormed out of the room.

＊＊＊

Lana left Clifford behind and attended the Merchants' Guild's regular meeting alone. Afterward, she was summoned by Seymour, one of the board members.

Seymour was a portly old gentleman and one of the leading great merchants in Sazandol. As befitted a board member, he was quite the shrewd businessman, but the way he smiled warmly at Lana gave him an approachable air.

"Actually, Miss Colette, there's something I'd very much like to discuss with you."

The boardroom had been cleared of everyone else. Lana had expected that this must be about business, but what came out of Seymour's mouth was something she had never anticipated.

"Would you consider becoming the wife of my son, Warren?"

You've got to be kidding me. That was her honest reaction.

(Can something like this really happen...? To receive two marriage proposals in just a few hours...)

To be honest, it wasn't as though she had never received such offers in the past. Her family was fabulously wealthy. There were plenty of people who wanted to marry Lana in the hope of establishing ties with the Colette Trading Company.

However, once Lana started running her own trading company, things changed.

If she were to manage one of the Colette Trading Company's branches alongside her husband, that would be fine. She would remain under the protection of the Colette family. But if Lana became independent and established a new trading company, there was always the possibility that it might become a rival to the Colette Trading Company. In that case, there would be little benefit to marrying her.

As far as Lana was concerned, her family's Colette Trading Company was not an adversary, but someone she respected and aspired to emulate. The difference in scale between them was simply that great. However, it seemed that outsiders viewed her as a selfish young lady who had rebelled against her parents and run away from home to start a trading company as a hobby.

For these reasons, the number of people proposing to Lana had recently dwindled.

And yet, today alone, she had received successive marriage proposals: first from her childhood friend Clifford, and now from Warren, the son of Board Member Seymour. To be honest, Lana was at a loss.

"Board Member, does this mean you want me to become a member of the Seymour Trading Company?"

"That is correct. I have high expectations of your abilities. What do you think?"

In other words, he wanted her to close down the Flax Trading Company and support the Seymour Trading Company as his son Warren's wife.

The Seymour Trading Company was not particularly short on money. In other words, this proposal wasn't based on any expectation of financial support from Lana's family. He was making the offer because he genuinely valued Lana's individual talents.

"You're very capable. You graduated from Serendia Academy, and you have connections with people in high society. I wouldn't hesitate to entrust one of our branches to you."

This was less a marriage proposal than a business proposition. In fact, that was probably exactly how Seymour saw it. He was trying to poach Lana, and marriage was an effective bargaining chip when it came to recruiting someone away from another company.

"To put it bluntly, it isn't easy for an unmarried young lady to continue serving as the head of a trading company... I'm sure you've felt that keenly yourself, haven't you?"

His voice wasn't overbearing. If anything, it carried a note of sympathy.

What Seymour said was true. Female heads of trading companies were rare, and a woman's social credibility differed enormously depending on whether she was married or unmarried. As Seymour had said, unmarried women were treated as "naive young ladies."

Lana also understood just how important trust was in business.

She had been casually brushed aside more than once or twice by people who claimed that a trading company run by an unmarried young lady couldn't possibly be trusted.

"We'll also take care of the employees of your Flax Trading Company. If any of them wish, I can even write letters of introduction to other workshops. Won't you please give this offer some serious consideration?"

He valued Lana's abilities and was even willing to look after the future of the Flax Trading Company's employees. It was an extremely generous offer.

It certainly wasn't a bad deal, but even so, Lana couldn't bring herself to accept.

To begin with, she had no intention of closing down the Flax Trading Company. Her company had only just gotten started.

"I'm very sorry."

As Lana bowed deeply, Seymour gave a small sigh.

"I see... What a shame. My son was quite enthusiastic about the idea."

"I'm sure there is someone far more suitable for Warren-sama than me."

"Well, I certainly hope so..."

His murmured words were tinged with bitterness and sorrow.

＊＊＊

When Lana returned, Rick, the coachman who had been waiting beside the carriage, looked at her face and spoke with concern.

"Are you tired, Miss?"

"Oh, do I look that exhausted?"

Of everyone at the Flax Trading Company, Rick had known Lana the longest. Having known her back when she still lived at her family home, he apparently thought she looked worn out.

Rick was lanky and tall, and at a glance, he looked about the same age as Lana. In reality, however, he was an eleven-year-old boy. A spirit had been possessing him for many years, causing his body to grow faster than those of the other children around him.

Feeling embarrassed to be fussed over by a boy like that, Lana lightly pinched her cheeks.

"Just a little. I've had a lot going on lately."

The succession of marriage proposals had left her feeling rather exasperated, but it was also true that work had been piling up.

At the moment, the biggest event on her agenda was the unveiling of a new robe at the Magical Technology Exhibition.

As its name suggested, the Magical Technology Exhibition showcased magical technology—including sorcery—as well as devices and other technologies that made use of magical power. Lana intended to exhibit robes designed for sorcerers and promote them to magic schools and people involved in magical technology.

The exhibition had originally been scheduled for the beginning of the new year, but a major incident had occurred in early winter during the month of Shelgria at Cressburn Magical University, which was famous for its magical technology. As a result, the exhibition had been postponed by a month.

In other words, they still had time to make adjustments to the robes they had prepared.

(Still, lately, the employees have been getting into arguments with each other more often, and it's giving me a headache.)

At the Flax Trading Company, Lana had ten artisans working under Bartholomeus, the workshop chief. Half of them were experienced artisans introduced by Lana's father—the so-called referral group. The other half were artisans Lana had found and hired in Sazandol—the local group.

The referral group consisted of skilled veterans, just as one would expect from artisans her father had personally taken under his wing. The local group, whom Lana had recruited herself, consisted mostly of young people with promising futures.

The two groups were at odds over the production of the robes. The referral group in particular had many artisans with strong pride in their work, and even Bartholomeus, the workshop chief, was having trouble dealing with them.

(As the head of the company, I need to handle this firmly...)

In particular, some of the artisans in the referral group openly looked down on Lana. They treated her exactly as Board Member Seymour had just described: as a mere young lady.

Remembering her exchange with Board Member Seymour made her brow furrow involuntarily. Wrinkles between her brows weren't cute. As Lana vigorously smoothed them out with her fingertips, a voice rang out behind her.

"Good afternoon, Lana-san. Have you heard from my father?"

The person approaching her with a smile was none other than Warren Seymour, the very man whose marriage proposal had just come up. He was a little older than Lana, a handsome young man with brilliant blond hair. His clothes were tasteful and fashionable, too. He was clearly a stylish gentleman at a glance.

"Good afternoon, Mr. Seymour. If you're referring to that matter..."

Before Lana could tell him that she had just turned it down, Warren took her hand.

"Actually, I should have told you myself before my father spoke to you. Would you please become my wife?"

"U-um..."

Warren smiled pleasantly. It was the confident smile characteristic of someone who had found success in business.

"I've been interested in you for some time. You're so lively, and you look adorable when you smile. You also have a good sense of style."

"I'm honored."

"Above all, I'm certain that with someone as intelligent as you, we could make my father's business even bigger."

At Warren's words, Rick turned deathly pale and began trembling.

"Miss...? Wife...? With that man...?"

Without taking her eyes off Warren, Lana answered Rick behind her.

"I already declined that offer."

For just an instant, Warren's expression vanished. His eyes went cold in that moment. Lana had seen that look many times at Serendia Academy.

It was the look of someone who approached her in a friendly manner but had no intention of treating her as an equal, looking down on a naive, nouveau riche young lady.

...That said, it was only natural for someone to be upset when their marriage proposal was rejected. Lana bowed her head modestly, hoping to avoid making a scene.

"It's far too generous an offer for someone like me. I'm sure there is someone much more suitable for you, Mr. Seymour."

"There's no need to rush your answer, Lana-san. Why don't we take our time getting to know each other first...?"

"Wouldn't it be dishonest to put off giving you an answer? Dishonesty is a 'disgrace' to both nobles and merchants."

She had deliberately added "to nobles" because Lana's father was a baron. It was an indirect reminder that their families were of different social standings, since Warren's family held no title.

It wasn't admirable to flaunt one's status, but Lana knew how to use her rank as a weapon when she needed to keep someone in check.

(It's not that I dislike this man.)

Warren was probably ambitious. Lana felt no particular aversion to that. She herself was ambitious, wanting to succeed as a merchant.

She just had no intention of abandoning the Flax Trading Company and marrying him for the sake of his ambitions. That was all there was to it.

"Rick, please get the carriage ready."

Taking advantage of the moment when Warren fell silent, Lana called out to Rick.

"Good day, Mr. Seymour."

After saying only that, she climbed into the carriage. Rick immediately leaped into the driver's seat and set the carriage in motion.

Lana opened the window leading to the driver's seat and called out to Rick.

"I'm sorry for making you worry about me, Rick."

"No, not at all! Um, Miss, is there anywhere you'd like to go? I'll take you anywhere! I'll drive as safely as I possibly can!"

"Hehe. Then, could you make a little detour to Monica's house?"

"Yes, Miss!"

Monica, who had been staying in the royal capital for the New Year's ceremonies, should be returning to Sazandol soon.

Lana wanted to catch up with her over a meal after so long apart. And she would laugh off the fact that she'd received two marriage proposals in a single day, with a few complaints thrown in for good measure.

＊＊＊

How long had it been since the hand that had been writing came to a stop?

Monica noticed that the hand she'd been resting her cheek on had grown slightly numb, and lifted her chin back into place. The nib of the quill in her right hand had gone completely dry.

She had only just returned to her home in Sazandol the other day, but somehow she couldn't get into the swing of things. Even when she looked at the documents for her research, the numbers wouldn't sink in. It was the first time she'd ever had trouble processing numbers, of all things, even though the words themselves were no problem.

Before she knew it, she'd stopped moving her quill and was thinking about the party hosted by the Starseer Witch instead.

That day, Monica had played the part of a villainess to bring the Abyss Shaman, Ray Albright, and his fiancée together. That part had gone well enough, but at the very last moment, her dress had come undone, and things had turned into quite a disaster.

Monica had changed clothes immediately and slipped out of the party venue without meeting anyone. Her thoughts were in complete disarray, and she didn't want to face anyone.

Then, after returning to Sazandol, Monica remembered something. She failed to congratulate Cyril on his engagement.

(I missed my chance to say it...)

She knew she had to say it properly the next time they met... but for some reason, the thought weighed heavily on her. Her heart felt unsettled.

On top of that, whenever she thought about Cyril, she also remembered the moment her dress had come undone, and the embarrassment made her want to disappear.

Would talking to someone help take her mind off things? But Isaac had returned to the Duchy of Erin, and Nero was hibernating. Her home, which had been empty for the first time in a while, felt terribly quiet and cold.

(...Maybe I'll have some coffee.)

Monica stood up and headed for the kitchen, but then remembered that she was out of coffee beans. In that case, she decided to have some tea instead and reached for a teacup in the cupboard.

However, when things aren't going well, nothing seems to go right.

Her fingertips, numb from the cold, couldn't get a proper grip on the cup. It slipped from Monica's hand and fell to the floor.

She didn't even have time to use chantless magic before the sound of shattering porcelain rang out.

"...Ah."

At her feet lay the cup, shattered into countless pieces. It had a delicate floral pattern on a white background, and was one of the cups Lana had given her as a housewarming present when Monica moved to Sazandol.

And now it was broken.

Monica could use all sorts of complicated sorcery, but she couldn't put a shattered teacup back together.

A broken teacup could never return to the way it was before.

"..."

The moment her precious teacup shattered, something that had been stretched taut inside Monica finally snapped.

All the emotions she'd been bottling up for so long came pouring out, and teardrops fell onto the fragments of the teacup.

"Ugh... hic... ugh~~~... No..."

What was that little, murmured "no" directed at? The broken cup, or something else...?

Monica stifled her sobs and began gathering the pieces of the teacup. She felt that if she let herself make any more noise, she would burst into loud, childish sobs.

Just then, the sound of someone knocking on the door echoed through the house.

"Monica! You're back, aren't you? Let's go out for lunch together!"

It was Lana's voice. Oh, what kind of face was she supposed to show Lana?

Monica wiped away her tears and tried to stand, but stepped on the hem of her skirt and fell to the floor. The pieces of the teacup she'd gathered scattered everywhere. One of them sliced deeply into the palm of Monica's left hand. Red blood slowly welled up from the cut.

Honestly, everything that could possibly go wrong had gone wrong.

"Ugh... ugh... whaaah..."

As she lay on the floor, sniffling and sobbing, Lana opened the door and rushed inside.

"Hey! I just heard a tremendous noise! Are you all right?!"

When Lana saw Monica lying on the floor, her eyes widened in shock.

"Monica, are you okay?! Oh no, your hand is bleeding! Are you hurt anywhere else?"

"Hic, wah... Lana, s-sorry... I... broke the teacup... I'm so sorry..."

As Monica lay face-down on the floor, crying like a little child, Lana gently stroked her back.

Then she took out a handkerchief and wrapped it around Monica's injured hand.

"Don't cry so much. Come on, calm down."

"But... hic... ugh..."

"Walker-san isn't home today, is he? Anyway, let's wash your hand first."

"O-okay."

＊＊＊

While briskly cleaning up the broken pieces of the teacup, Lana glanced over at Monica as she washed her hands.

(...Something must have happened.)

The Monica she hadn't seen in so long looked visibly worn out. She was wearing nothing but a well-worn robe with a stole draped over it, and her hair was a mess, with strands sticking out of her braids.

Monica was the type of person whose emotional distress showed plainly in her appearance. The more troubled she was, the more careless she tended to become about her personal grooming.

"Here, sit down. Let's put some medicine on your hand. Do you have a medicine box?"

"Um, on that shelf..."

The shelf Monica pointed to was neatly organized. Her capable disciple must have been keeping it tidy on a regular basis. Thanks to that, Lana found the medicine box right away.

Fortunately, the wound on Monica's palm wasn't particularly deep. Lana applied medicine to her hand and covered it with a bandage.

"There, all done. Don't move that hand around too much today."

"...Okay."

Monica remained seated, her head hanging as she gazed absently at her bandaged left hand.

After putting away the medicine box, Lana crouched down beside Monica's chair. Resting her cheek on her own knee, she looked up at Monica.

"Did something happen while we weren't seeing each other?"

Monica bit her chapped lips and fell silent, as though searching for the right words.

Eventually, she slowly opened her mouth.

"Lately, I'm just... completely useless."

"What makes you useless?"

When Lana asked in a gentle voice, Monica closed her swollen eyes, as if reflecting on her own behavior.

"I broke the cup you gave me, and I keep knocking over pots and pen holders..."

"Mm-hmm."

"I can't concentrate on my research, and I even make mistakes with simple addition..."

"Mm-hmm."

"And I couldn't properly congratulate Cyril-sama on his engagement..."

"...Hmm?"

The fact that Monica had made a mistake calculating something was already fairly shocking, but this was an even more startling topic.

Lana's expression stiffened as she repeated the words.

"Ashley-sama is engaged?"

"...I heard at the party the other day that there was a marriage proposal involving the daughter of the Marquis of Bersting..."

This was the first Lana had heard of it. She often visited salons where gossipy ladies gathered, but she had never heard anything about a marriage proposal involving Cyril.

Still, Monica's attendance at the party had been a recent event, so it was reasonable to assume that her information was more up to date.

(And what terrible timing...)

Just when Lana had been about to vent about her own marriage proposals, it turned out that something similar had happened to Cyril as well. The timing couldn't have been worse.

(The daughter of the Marquis of Bersting... If I remember correctly, she's related to the Starseer Witch-sama. It wouldn't be surprising if there were marriage talks...)

Never take rumors at face value unless they come from a reliable source. That was a fundamental rule of business.

As Lana considered how best to gather information, she observed Monica's condition.

Monica was visibly depressed. Surely it wasn't just because she'd broken the teacup.

(Does she even realize why the news of Ashley-sama's engagement has made her so upset?)

Lana let out a weary sigh and stood up.

Whether or not the news of Cyril's engagement was true, her priority right now was cheering up the depressed Monica.

"Monica, I'm going to teach you what to do when absolutely nothing goes right!"

"What to do...?"

Monica looked at Lana with pleading eyes, like a patient being told about a cure for an incurable disease.

Lana pointed a finger sharply at Monica's forehead.

"When nothing goes right, you dress up as fabulously as you can, go shopping, and buy yourself something you like! Then you eat your fill of delicious fried pies!"

At the Colette-style solution for a nouveau riche young lady, Monica's eyes widened. "...Fried... pies?" she murmured, looking puzzled. Apparently, she'd never tried one before.

"Eating too many fried sweets will make you gain weight, but... this is a special occasion! You have to spoil yourself absolutely rotten!"

Monica still looked like she didn't quite understand. Being the serious-minded person she was, the idea of indulging herself at a time like this probably didn't come naturally to her.

It wasn't something she could exactly be proud of, but Lana was very good at spoiling herself.

Why not take this opportunity to teach Monica the art of pampering herself?

"Now then! First, once the swelling around your eyes has gone down, we'll change your clothes and put on some makeup!"
